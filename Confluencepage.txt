## 1. Executive Summary

UC0008395 is an AI-enabled use case under WS5 of the Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) programme. Its objective is to improve the quality, consistency and efficiency of third-party continuity and recoverability assessments by using Generative AI to consolidate information from structured inputs and unstructured artefacts.

The proposed solution is embedded within the existing third-party resilience assessment workflow. It generates a standardized first-draft assessment report and highlights potential gaps for analyst review. Human reviewers remain responsible for validating the AI-generated output, refining risk ratings and completing the final report.

The use case has progressed through Ideation experimentation and POC. The POC governance request has been completed, and the team has submitted the Pilot governance request, including the newly introduced feeder form. The next milestone is to obtain the required Pilot governance approvals, resolve any outstanding review conditions, and confirm that the Pilot request reaches the required approved/closed-complete state.

Production governance will be treated as a separate lifecycle stage and must be completed before Production deployment is considered approved.

## 2. Business Context and Problem Statement

### 2.1 Business problem

Third-party continuity and recoverability assessments require information to be collected and reviewed across multiple systems, documents and reporting sources.

The current process faces the following challenges:

* Information is distributed across different systems and may be disconnected, inconsistent or misaligned.
* Third-party continuity and recoverability plans may not always be up to date or reliable.
* Assessment preparation is manual and user-intensive.
* Inconsistent application of the assessment process can affect the completeness and consistency of reports.
* Analysts spend time gathering and consolidating information that could be prepared more efficiently.

### 2.2 Proposed AI solution

The proposed solution uses Generative AI to:

1. Consolidate structured inputs and unstructured supporting artefacts.
2. Process relevant information for third-party resilience assessments.
3. Generate a standardized first-draft assessment report.
4. Highlight potential gaps and provide prompts for analyst review.
5. Support analysts in validating information, refining risk ratings and preparing the final report.

The solution is intended to assist the existing business process rather than remove the need for analyst review and accountability.

### 2.3 Business success criteria

The intended outcomes include:

* Faster and more accurate third-party resilience assessments.
* Improved consistency and completeness of assessment outputs.
* Better-informed risk management and decision-making.
* Greater confidence in the bank's ability to limit disruption to key services and recover following incidents.
* Reduced manual effort and resources required to support the assessment process.

**Benefits measurement:** The ServiceNow supporting-benefits record indicates that the design work is ongoing and the benefit metrics are to be updated. Quantified productivity gains, time savings and realized financial benefits should not be reported until they have been validated.

WS5 is one of the programme's core workstreams and focuses on improving the active risk management of third-party continuity planning and recoverability confidence.

## 5. Ideation Environment – Activities Completed

### 5.1 Platform access and project setup

The team used the Group AI Platform Ideation capability to become familiar with the platform and explore model suitability for the use case.

The hands-on activities included:

* Creating the platform project named **Group AI Platform Hands-On POC**.
* Adding use case UC0008395 to the project.
* Selecting Basic Access for initial experimentation.
* Exploring the available model subscription and Playground functionality.
* Submitting a subscription request for **QWEN3 LLM version 1 (Chat)** in the CNI location.
* Receiving approval for that model subscription.
* Performing an initial Playground test using a TCPAR-related question.

### 5.2 Purpose of the Ideation work

The Ideation work helped the team understand:

* How projects and use cases are associated in the platform.
* How model subscriptions are requested and approved.
* How to experiment with an available model using the Playground.
* How to assess whether the model's responses are relevant to the business problem.
* The difference between Basic Access/Playground experimentation and API access required for application integration.

### 5.3 Observations and limitations

The initial Playground test demonstrated that the model could produce a response, but the response was not independently validated against the actual assessment report or an authoritative business source.

Therefore, the Playground experiment should be treated as an initial feasibility exercise, not evidence of business accuracy or production readiness.

Basic Access supports Playground experimentation but does not, by itself, provide API invocation capability. API integration requires the appropriate environment, authentication and access arrangements.

**Environment-specific note:** The Ideation experiment used QWEN3 LLM version 1 (Chat), while the POC/Pilot governance records list Azure-hosted models. These refer to different recorded platform activities. The final target model configuration should be confirmed against the actual implementation and the latest governance submission.
